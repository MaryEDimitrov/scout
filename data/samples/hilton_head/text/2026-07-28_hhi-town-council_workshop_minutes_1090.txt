Workshop Discussion
Review and Discussion of Proposed William Hilton Parkway Gateway Corridor Master Plan
Mayor Perry began the meeting by stating that the Workshop Meeting is taking place to
address traffic issues within the corridor and to ensure everyone will have a clear
understanding of how to move forward, so a plan can be delivered to Council in August for a
Resolution in support of Town staff in order to begin working on obtaining funding and moving
the process forward.
Mr. Orlando recalled that the last time Council was together was April 28, 2026, as they worked
on the Gateway Corridor Master Plan (Master Plan), and this Workshop is taking place as a
continuation of project planning and to hear continued feedback on the work itself.
Mr. Orlando reminded Town Council that he was directed to advance a coordinated Master
Planning effort for the William Hilton Gateway Corridor Project. As a result of the coordinated
Town Council and Beaufort County efforts on the use of the 2018 Beaufort County
Transportation Referendum proceeds, all of which were allocated to the eastbound bridge,
Town Council is left with a project plan in place and a Master Plan expectation to advance.

Key Points Discussed:
• 2025 vs. 2030 Levels of Service: staff clarified that color changes (green/yellow/red)
represent shifts in traffic performance after proposed improvements. Some quadrants
show limited improvement, while others, especially movements involving Squire Pope
and Chamberlin, show clearer benefits.
• Continuous Green T Design: this configuration removes one signal phase, improving
flow for on-island movements and reducing conflict points. A pedestrian bridge would be
required because pedestrians cannot safely cross where on-island traffic is no longer
signalized.
• Lane Configuration Questions: council members noted inconsistencies regarding added
lanes shown in the slides - some depicted a third lane that isn’t part of the 2030 model.
Mr. Turner clarified that certain long-term lane additions fall outside the 2030 analysis
horizon.
• Traffic Volume Data Differences: Council questioned discrepancies between DOT’s
average daily counts and a Planning Commission three-day July count showing a 68/32
directional split. Mr. Turner explained these reflect different time periods and collection
methods; both sets can be accurate for their respective measurement windows.
• Crash Data at Spanish Wells & Wild Horse: this intersection has the highest crash
density on the corridor. Rear-end collisions are common due to congestion and merging
patterns from Cross Island Parkway. Short-term improvements primarily increase
capacity (e.g., dual left turn lanes), while long-term designs aim to reduce conflict points,
significantly improving safety.
• Short-Term vs. Long-Term Improvements: Council emphasized the need for clarity on
what improvements are included in short-term plans versus future grade-separation
concepts, especially regarding lane additions and environmental impacts (e.g., wetland
concerns). Mr. Turner stated that, based on current information, short-term work is not
expected to impact wetlands.
• Requests for More Detail: Multiple council members asked for quantitative measures of
how much throughput improves (not just color-coded Lane of Service); capacity changes
from added lanes; expected safety gains; and comparisons between short-term fixes and
long-term grade-separated solutions.
Mr. Kinzelman then provided a proposed Summary of Improvements:
• Improve level of service and vehicular thru-put at problematic intersections with posed
speed reduced to 40 mph.
• Enhance pedestrian pathways and create better connectivity in the corridor.
• Provide streetscape improvements (landscape, medians, grading, signage, art).
• Reconfigure Squire Pope Road/Chamberlin Drive intersection to support continuous on-
island traffic flow.
• Reconfigure Wild Horse Road/Spanish Wells Road intersection to support continuous
on-island traffic flow.
• Establish Gullah Geechee Heritage Park.
• Construct additional eastbound travel lane.

Mr. Kinzelman completed the presentation by providing information for additional corridor

additional transportation system improvement projects for locations around Hilton Head Island.
Mr. Orlando redirected Council in connection with projects that the Town could focus on with
respect to making recommendations to SCDOT and Beaufort County. He highlighted that Hilton
Head’s major traffic issues - capacity, accidents, congestion, and neighborhood impacts - can
only be addressed through a small set of feasible solutions while staying within existing
right-of-way limits. Key intersections at Spanish Wells Road and Squire Pope Road require
either at-grade or elevated improvements. Recommendations to SCDOT include evaluating
continuous flow near Windmill Harbor, restriping Cross Island Parkway, and improving signage
and lane markings. Mr. Orlando stressed the need for full-corridor upgrades on Gum Tree
Road, school-zone safety improvements, speed reductions, and better pedestrian
infrastructure; Main Street needs sidewalks, crosswalks, and improved traffic geometry.
Additional focus areas such as Palmetto Bay Road, Arrow Road, and Sea Pines Circle require
enhanced signal timing and safety work. All these efforts align with the Town’s Capital
Improvement Program and rely on coordinated action with SCDOT and Beaufort County.
Council members and staff discussed ongoing transportation planning for the US 278 corridor,
focusing heavily on whether the current Continuous Green T and grade-separation concepts
adequately address long-term traffic flow, safety, and community impacts.
Key points included:
• A request for the County to create a west-end bicycle path connection to the bridge to
complement a planned east-end connection.
• Safety concerns were emphasized: eliminating signals could introduce higher-speed rear-end
and merge collisions, particularly at Spanish Wells Road and Squire Pope Road.
• A suggestion was made for exploring alternative concepts, including a full frontage road,
grade separations, and flyovers, to improve economic development opportunities in the Stoney
neighborhood.
• The Town Manager reminded Council that the project does not start from a blank slate, noting
prior community commitments, property constraints, and the need for cooperation with the
County and State.
• Council discussed whether potential changes should be incorporated into the upcoming
Resolution and long-term project list, including adding a future frontage road project for
continued exploration.
• Concern was expressed about shifting direction late in the process, emphasizing community
trust, particularly from the Stoney neighborhood, which had long been vocal during earlier
phases, and clarity is needed to relay that such solutions would require the willingness of all
parties involved.
• Council expressed support for moving the current plan forward while ensuring future ideas
can still be explored and with an emphasis on maintaining strong cooperation with partner
agencies.

Mayor Perry asked if there was public comment.
Richard Bisi addressed Council regarding the cost of the project at $126 million dollars and
whether these improvements reduce congestion and travel times for vehicles using the corridor
every day and if not, is the cost of the project actually buying improvements and what
alternatives might be considered. Mr. Bisi remarked that Senator Davis's expectation was that
the Master Plan should be finalized by March 31, 2025, so the Town is behind schedule, the
process lacks transparency and the Plan does not demonstrate how it will improve daily traffic.
He also expressed concern that the Town does not have the money for the project and that
there is a need for enforcement on 278 to reduce speeding.
Skip Hoagland addressed Council suggesting the use of ATAX funds for more police
protection and infrastructure that benefits tourists and local residents; that the Destination
Marketing Organization is not needed as AI should be used to generate marketing for tourism
for free and to run government more efficiently and suggested instituting a tourist impact fee
which could offset costs for the corridor project.
Dave Ferguson addressed Council to relay concern that the decision-making process for the
278 corridor is taking too long and why consensus among the Mayor and Council wasn't built
sooner and suggested meeting with County and State officials to figure out where those offices
stand concerning the project before further voting occurs.
Peter Kristian addressed Council to relay that he manages Hilton Head Plantation and sees
that his residents experience difficulty with the traffic conditions to get to and from service
providers that are off-Island and that some vendors will no longer provide services for Island
residents due to the fact that it is so difficult to get on and off the Island. He also relayed that
parents have a safety concern in getting children to school on Main Street, so a safe
passageway should be a priority. He also mentioned the possibility of the Town purchasing
some properties on the north side of 278 to ensure the creation of a right of way and is also
concerned for public safety if it is necessary to evacuate residents.
Christopher Cliffe addressed Council to thank them and the consultants for the presentation
and for their willingness to answer questions; he expressed that he doesn't feel that SCDOT
and Beaufort County are reliable partners, as they have their own agenda and depending on
their cooperation, will result in the Town having to compromise the proposal for improvements
and it might be in the Town's best interest if there are some proposals to improve safety and
reduce congestion that can be moved forward without the input of the SCDOT and Beaufort
County, especially because he feels that SCDOT traffic data is outdated and unreliable.

Marc Orlando, Town Manager, thanked the Mayor, staff, consultants, and community members
for their long-term input. He clarified that several major projects — Main Street, Squire Pope
Road, Spanish Wells Road, Gum Tree Road, Sea Pines Circle, and the William Hilton Parkway
Gateway Corridor — are already included in the Town’s Capital Improvement Program and are
actively being advanced. He noted delays stem partly from misunderstandings: the Town began
corridor work only after Beaufort County redirected $80 million in referendum funds solely to the
bridge. Current 2024–2025 traffic data meets DOT standards. Mr. Orlando addressed short-
term improvements, including elevating Chamberlin Drive’s priority and exploring Master Plan
features that could bring added community benefits. He discussed traffic flow issues around
Squire Pope and Gum Tree Roads, potential right in/right out adjustments, and improved
westbound access from the Cross Island Parkway with speed enforcement. He reaffirmed his
commitment to pursuing funding once Council sets direction, including work already underway
on a potential TIF district with Beaufort County and the school district to support Safe Routes to
School and key intersections. He outlined the next steps: Council resolution, identifying funding,
project planning, ongoing CIP updates, and continued coordination with County and school
p artners.
