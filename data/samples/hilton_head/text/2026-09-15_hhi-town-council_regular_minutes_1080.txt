Beaufort County Update on Eastbound US 278 Bridge Replacement Project - Jared Fralix,
Assistant County Administrator, Beaufort County
Jared Fralix, Beaufort County Assistant County Administrator - Infrastructure, provided an
update on the status of the William Hilton Parkway Gateway Corridor project, which began in
2017 and shifted after rising construction costs required the original plan to be scaled back
from a full corridor redesign to a focused replacement of the aging 1956 eastbound Mackay
Creek Bridge. The revised plan maintains essential access points such as Hog Cove and
Mariners Cove, and improved access to Pinckney Island National Wildlife Refuge and the C.C.
Haigh, Jr. Boat Landing, avoids impacts to the Stoney area, and is designed to support future
expansion when the westbound bridges eventually require replacement.
The project is currently in final design and permitting following a federal Finding of No
Significant Impact (FONSI), which gives permission to move forward on right of way acquisition
and utility coordination, which is underway. The expectation is to have a final design complete
by summer of 2027, for the project to go out for bid in the fall and winter of 2027, with
construction anticipated to begin in early 2028 and finish by mid to late 2030.
Discussions are ongoing about relocating and funding the jointly owned Hilton Head PSD and
Broad Creek PSD water pipeline. Engineering analyses indicate the pipeline should be moved
for safety reasons, but the South Carolina Department of Transportation maintains that
relocation is unnecessary and may be considered an unreimbursable betterment. Following an
initial denial of a request for a U.S. Department of Transportation Large Bridge Project Grant, a
revised submittal for the grant was made and is pending, and an award could trigger
reconsideration of the larger corridor plan.
For up-to-date information, visit: info@SCDOT278BridgeReplacement.com

Consideration of a Resolution by the Town of Hilton Head Island, South Carolina Adopting the
William Hilton Parkway Gateway Corridor Master Plan and Other Matters Related Thereto -
Shawn Gillen, Special Projects Director
Shawn Leininger, Deputy Town Manager, provided an overview of the item.
Mr. Leininger outlined the William Hilton Parkway Gateway Corridor Master Plan, emphasizing
that its purpose is to establish a shared long-term vision and policy framework to guide future
infrastructure decisions along the Corridor. The Master Plan was developed through extensive
data analysis, coordination with state and county partners, and substantial community
engagement, including input from the Stoney Historic Neighborhood and Town Council
Workshops held on April 28, 2026, and July 28, 2026. It focuses on improving safety, mobility,
environmental stewardship, neighborhood connectivity, economic vitality, and the preservation
of community character while ensuring no private property impacts. Key recommendations

include redesigned intersections, continuous flow traffic strategies, pedestrian and bicycle
connectivity enhancements, potential new bridges, and corridor beautification. The Master
Plan also presents phased project sequencing, identifies near-term high impact improvements,
and outlines long-term major projects requiring substantial permitting and funding.
Adoption of the Master Plan does not authorize construction but instead allows the Town
Manger to:
• Utilize the Master Plan as the framework for future coordination with Beaufort County,
SCDOT, federal agencies, property owners, and other project partners.
• Advance improvements in phases based upon documented safety needs, operational
deficiencies, community benefit, funding availability, and constructibility.
• Require subsequent engineering and project development to demonstrate the specific public
benefit and need for each major improvement.
• Return to Town Council with funding strategies, sources, and planning-level cost estimates
before individual capital projects are authorized.
Adoption of the Master Plan provides a coordinated framework for addressing transportation,
safety, connectivity, and neighborhood needs along with the South Carolina Department of
Transportation and Beaufort County, South Carolina's advancement of the U.S. 278 eastbound
bridge replacement project.
The Master Plan identifies twelve potential projects with a combined planning-level estimate of
$125.8 million, based on 2025 costs. Five projects, totaling an estimated $27.6 million, were
identified as priorities for immediate planning, engineering, funding and intergovernmental
coordination:
• Spanish Wells Road/Wild Horse Road intersection improvements, estimated at $4.3 million.
• Continuous Green-T at Squire Pope Road/Chamberlin Drive, estimated at $900,000.
• Pedestrian bridge west of Squire Pope Road/Chamberlin Drive, estimated at $14.1 million.
• Chamberlin Drive extension to Spanish Wells Road, estimated at $3.6 million.
• Old Wild Horse Road streetscape improvements, estimated at $4.7 million.
The remaining seven projects are longer-term concepts that will be considered as corridor
needs, funding opportunities and partnerships develop:
• Development of a Gullah Geechee Heritage Park in alignment with Old Schoolhouse Park.
• Construction of a two-lane bridge with a pathway and new roundabouts at the Spanish Wells
Road/Wild Horse Road intersection.
• Intersection and traffic signal mast-arm improvements at Crosstree Drive/C. Heinrichs Circle
near Windmill Harbor.
• Corridor-wide pathway, landscaping, wayfinding and placemaking improvements.
• A Jenkins Island access road, parking areas, and improvements to the historic
Jenkins Island Cemetery.
• Construction of an additional eastbound travel lane from the bridge area to the Squire Pope

Consideration of an Ordinance Approving Amendments to Town Procurement Code Section
11-1-315, Small Purchases - First Reading - Doug Broyles, Chief Procurement Officer
Doug Broyles, Chief Procurement Officer, provided a brief summary.
The ordinance represents a proposed update to the Town Code’s (Town Code Section 11-1-
315) small purchase threshold, recommending changes to modernize procurement limits
originally set in 2007. Staff noted that current procedures allow departments to directly make
purchases under $7,500, require quotes or existing contracts for purchases up to $25,000, and
require public solicitation above that amount. The recommendation increases departmental
authority to purchases under $10,000 and raises the small purchase limit to $50,000, citing
inflation, benchmarking against other municipalities, and the Town’s average purchase cost of
just over $26,000. The change would also improve access for local small businesses that may
not be positioned to respond to formal solicitations.
The recommendation to advance the ordinance to Town Council for consideration was
reviewed, and unanimously approved 3-0, by the Finance & Administration Committee in their
regular meeting on Monday, August 10th, 2026.

Consideration of a Resolution Authorizing the Town Manager to Execute Certain Documents
Associated with Wetland Mitigation for the Crossings Park Project - Don D'Adam, Public
Projects Program Director
Don D'Adam, Public Projects Program Director, provided a brief summary of the item.
The resolution authorizes the Town Manager to execute a Wetland and Stream Mitigation
Credit Purchase Agreement with Weyerhaeuser NR Company, as required for the Town of
Hilton Head Island’s Crossings Park Phase I project. The agreement represents one of the
final steps needed before the project can move forward to public advertisement.
The Finance and Administrative Committee previously reviewed the item on August 10, 2026,
voting unanimously (3–0) to forward it to Town Council for consideration.
Mr. Brown moved to approve. Ms. Brison seconded.
Mayor Perry asked for public comment. There was none.
Council members requested clarification on what the wetland mitigation process involved. Mr.
D'Adam explained that the project must follow permitting steps through the U.S. Army Corps of
Engineers and must meet the compensatory mitigation requirements of the South Carolina
Department of Environmental Services, Bureau of Coastal Management, Coastal Zone
Consistency section, which scores the impacted wetlands and determines the number of
mitigation credits required—in this case, 5.148 credits. These credits are purchased on the
open market through pre-qualified vendors such as Weyerhaeuser, with the associated
wetlands located in a different basin. The cost for the required freshwater wetland credits is
$84,942. Council members appreciated the explanation, noting the importance of making this
information clear to the public.

The resolution authorizes a formal Permit/Use Agreement between the Town of Hilton Head
Island and the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services (SCDES) for the
groundwater monitoring well (Well BFT-2055) located at 4 Castnet Drive near the Singleton
Beach parking lot. The state operates about 180 such wells statewide to track groundwater

for years under informal approval. The state is now standardizing agreements for all well sites,
and the proposed agreement—running through 2035—does not alter the well’s operation but
formalizes access and terms. The Permit/Use Agreement would allow SCDES employees to
