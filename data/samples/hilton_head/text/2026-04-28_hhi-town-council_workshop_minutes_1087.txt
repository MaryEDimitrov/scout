Beaufort Countv Update on William Hilton Parkwav Gatewav Corridor Bridqe Project
Mayor Perry introduced the Workshop Discussion and stated that this Workshop is addressing
the status ofthe scope of the base ofthe bridge from Windmill Harbor through Spanish Wells.
Mayor Perry then turned the meeting over to the Town Manager, Marc Orlando. Mr. Orlando
introduced Jared Fralix, Assistant County Administrator of Beaufort County.
Jared Fralix provided a presentation of the status of the William Hilton Parkway Gateway
Corridor Bridge Project and a brief to Town Council on the Federal Highway Administration
Large Bridge Grant application. Mr. Fralix provided an outline as to how a potential grant
award would allow for expansion of the project scope to include replacement of the westbound
bridge. Currently, the US278 bridge project scope focuses on replacing the eastbound bridge
entering Hilton Head Island in order to eliminate structural deficiencies and provide new bridge
and roadway approaches.
With regard to the expected timeline, Mr. Fralix relayed that as to design, the project is 60%
complete; environmental documents are being submitted with the Federal Highway
Administration, followed by right ofway acquisition with respect to Pinckney Island, final design
anticipated to be completed in the spring/summer of 2027 followed by requests for bids in the
fall/winter of 2027 and an expectation that a contractor will be procured with construction
beginning early 2028, and an expected two and a half year build for a summer of 2030 timeline

Review and Discussion of Proposed William Hilton Parkwav Gatewav Corridor Master Plan
Mr. Orlando began by stating that the William Hilton Parkway Gateway Corridor Master Plan
(the "Plan") reflects prior direction, coordination with Beaufort County and the SCOOT,
technical and engineering analysis and public comment and input and presents a path forward
which can be implemented. He also expressed that the Stoney Historic Neighborhood is a
primary consideration with regard to the Plan with a focus on safety, mobility and system
improvements and access while recognizing the community's cultural and historic significance.
The objective of the Workshop and presentation of the Plan is to obtain Town Council policy
direction to finalize the Plan and advance public engagement, funding, partnerships, and
implementation as well as to confirm alignment with Town Council priorities and to provide
direction on advancing the Plan into the next phase. This includes final refinement ofthe Plan,
initiation of broader public engagement, and development of a coordinated funding and
implementation strategy.
Mr. Orlando then turned the meeting over to Shawn Gillen, Assistant Town Manager, who
introduced and provided background on the team: Brian Kinzelman of MKSK, Jonathan Guy of
Kimley-Horn and Jeff Buckalew of Infrastructure Consulting and Engineering.
Mr. Kinzelman and Mr. Guy provided a presentation of the William Hilton Parkway Gateway
Corridor Master Plan which included the extent of the project, bridge project overview (as
previously presented by Jared Fralix), project objective along with guiding principles and
influences, a summary of the improvements, Jenkins Island improvements, Stoney
Neighborhood improvements, Old Wild Horse corridor improvements and an analysis ofthe
performance matrix, implementation sequence, project descriptions/cost estimates and
additional transportation system improvement projects.
The Guiding Principles (listed below) for the corridor plan were laid-out and specific data was
relayed for each Guiding Principle in order to convey an overall better understanding of the
Plan:
• Fix the transportation safety issues of the corridor
• Improve the safety and quality of life for residents, businesses and neighborhoods (in
particular, the Stoney Neighborhood) impacted by the corridor improvements
• Create a gateway to Hilton Head Island that the region will be proud of
• Improve the hurricane/evacuation route

Mayor Perry asked for public comment.
Donald Furtado addressed Council regarding his concern that the proposed US278
reconfiguration does not adequately address the US278/Squire Pope Road westbound
intersection and suggested a longer merge/acceleration lane to decrease collisions.
Skip Hoagland addressed Council to express his concern that the corridor improvements will
alleviate traffic congestion at the north end ofthe Island but not at the south end. He
expressed gratitude for the installment of the existing traffic light at the Windmill Harbor
intersection and suggested that a traffic loop at the intersection would further alleviate traffic,
and expressed his desire to have Windmill Harbor made a part of the Town of Hilton Head
Island.
Steven Baer addressed Council and expressed concern that the Plan is not cost-specific
enough and that it needed to include better traffic analysis. He also suggested that more
community input is needed and requested more opportunities for the community to work with
the project experts and offer suggestions and feedback.
Richard Bisi addressed Council regarding his concern that the corridor plan has not yet been
finalized and also suggested that transparency and community input could help in finalizing the
corridor plan.
Christopher Cliffe addressed Council and expressed that he was pleased by the presentation
but questioned whether SCOOT traffic data was used in connection with the Plan regarding
traffic on the Cross Island Parkway. Additionally, he expressed concern that the corridor plan
improvements will affect the cultural aspects of the Island relating to visitor impact, as well as
concern as to the cost of the improvements.
John Stewart addressed Council and expressed concern that the improvements would attract
more tourists to the Island and would not alleviate traffic congestion, in particular with regard to
the Stoney Neighborhood.
William Stewart addressed Council and expressed his concern that the Plan does not contain
enough specific data regarding improvement dimensions, start points and end points.
Tammy Gore addressed Council with concern that the current motor vehicle count (Average
Annual Daily Traffic Volumes) contained in the Plan may be insufficient considering Beaufort
County and Jasper County rates of growth and development.
Diederick Advocaat addressed Council regarding implementing a better traffic management
system.
